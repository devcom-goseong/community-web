About · Kyungdong University

A community built on purpose

Not a group chat that grew by accident. Before we opened the doors we agreed what this is, who it is for, how it is run and who is responsible for what — so that anyone joining knows exactly what they are joining.

How it started

On 26 August 2026, a small group of students at Kyungdong University sat down for a first planning meeting. The question on the table was simple: there are people here learning to build things, and no good way for them to find each other. What would it take to fix that properly?

The meeting produced a working plan rather than a slogan. It set out what had already been agreed, what still needed deciding, which activities the community should run, how it would be managed, who is responsible for each task, and what had to be finished before opening to a wider group.

That plan is still a working document. It gets updated as the community grows and as the team makes new decisions — which is the point.

What we are for

“The community is intended to welcome people with different levels of experience and different interests.” Meeting report · section 3.1

We want to be genuinely open and genuinely organised at the same time. Those two things pull against each other, and most student groups quietly give up on one of them. Our answer is a light joining process and a clear structure, so that being welcoming does not turn into being chaotic.

Before anyone joins, they should be able to find out:

  • What the community actually is
  • What they can expect from it
  • What is expected from them
  • What behaviour is acceptable
  • How activities are organised
  • How they can contribute
  • Who to contact when they need help

That is what this website is for. If something on it is unclear, that is a fault worth telling us about.

What we hold each other to

Respect first

No harassment, no discrimination, no manufactured conflict. Communication stays professional even when people disagree.

Questions are not embarrassing

Nobody should be afraid of being judged for asking something basic. A community where people stay quiet to look competent is worth nothing.

Credit is not optional

Respect other people's work. Attribute what you use. Never present someone else's work as your own.

Say who is doing what

Group projects need clear responsibilities from the start. Most collapses are not technical failures, they are unowned tasks.

Keep the channels clean

Announcements, discussions, project work and casual conversation live in separate places. Keeping them separate is how a community stays readable.

Leadership is a responsibility

It is not a title. It means taking something on when it needs doing. The community only continues if people are willing to contribute and take initiative.

How the community is organised

A core leadership and planning team is responsible for building and maintaining the community: the rules, the website, the communication platforms, the activities, the branding and the general organisation. As the community grows, more responsibilities and leadership positions will be created — the current shape is a starting point, not a ceiling.

  • A founding and leadership team sits at the centre.
  • It carries four areas: rules and structure; website and identity; platforms and channels; activities and events.
  • Those four areas exist to serve the membership, which is open to every skill level, technical or not.

How the work is divided

Four areas of responsibility, agreed at the founding meeting. They describe the work rather than the people doing it: members move between them, and an area can be held by one person or shared by several. Everyone on the team is expected to contribute where they can, because the community should not depend entirely on any one person.

Who can join, and how

Anyone at KDU who wants to learn or build something, at any level of experience, technical or not.

There is a short joining process. It exists so the community stays a place where people know what is happening — not to keep anyone out.

  1. You apply

    Fill in the form: who you are, what you are interested in, and why you want to join.

  2. We read it

    Someone on the founding team reviews it. Applications are read by a person, not filtered by a script.

  3. You get an invitation

    Accepted members receive an invitation to the community platform and the channels that suit them.

  4. You take part

    Come to a meeting, join a study group, submit an idea, or just listen for a while first.

The exact review and acceptance process is still being finalised. When it is fixed, it will be published here rather than kept in a private document.

The rules, in plain terms

The full text is on the community rules page, and reading it is part of applying. They are not meant to make the community strict — they are meant to prevent confusion later.

General behaviour
Respect between members. No harassment or discrimination. No unnecessary conflict. Communication stays professional and appropriate.
Participation
Respect for meetings and activities. No spam. Discussions stay relevant. People are encouraged to ask questions without fear of being judged.
Project work
Respect for other people's work, proper credit when you use it, no claiming someone else's work as your own, and clear responsibilities inside a group project.
Platforms
Proper use of each channel, appropriate content, and a clear separation between announcements, discussions, projects and casual conversation.
Leadership
What leaders are responsible for, how decisions get made, how members can propose improvements, and how disagreements are handled.
Changes
The rules are not permanent. As the community grows, the leadership team reviews them and improves them where necessary.

Now you know what you are joining.

If that sounds like something you want to be part of — or if you have a question about any of it — the same form gets you to us.
